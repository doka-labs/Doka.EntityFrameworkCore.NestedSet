# Code of Conduct

## Commitment

Doka Labs is committed to a professional, welcoming, and safe community. Every
person who participates in good faith receives the same respect, regardless of
identity, background, experience, role, or viewpoint.

## Expected Behavior

Community members are expected to:

- communicate respectfully and assume good intent while evidence is gathered;
- discuss technical decisions without attacking the people involved;
- give and receive constructive, specific feedback;
- acknowledge mistakes, repair harm where possible, and learn from incidents;
- respect expressed boundaries and the privacy of other people; and
- prioritize the safety and long-term health of the project community.

## Unacceptable Behavior

The following behavior is not accepted:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, or unwanted repeated contact;
- discriminatory, degrading, sexualized, or otherwise demeaning language or
  imagery;
- personal attacks, trolling, deliberate disruption, or sustained bad-faith
  participation;
- publishing another person's private information without explicit permission;
- retaliation against a reporter, witness, moderator, or affected person;
- impersonating another person or evading an enforcement action; or
- conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional
  technical community.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to NestedSet repository issues, pull requests,
discussions, reviews, commits, project-managed communication channels, and
events. It also applies when a person represents Doka Labs through an official
account, email address, role, or public appearance.

Technical disagreement, criticism of code, and rejection of a proposal are not
violations when expressed professionally and without targeting a person.

## Reporting an Incident

Report conduct concerns privately to `doka-labs@tuta.com` with the subject
`NestedSet Code of Conduct report`. Do not open a public issue for a conduct
incident.

Include, when available:

- what happened and when;
- links, screenshots, or other evidence;
- the people and project spaces involved;
- any immediate safety concern; and
- the outcome or protective measure being requested.

Security vulnerabilities follow the separate private process in
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md#reporting-a-vulnerability). General support requests
follow [SUPPORT.md](SUPPORT.md).

Reports are shared only with people needed to assess and resolve the incident.
The project targets acknowledgment within five business days and an initial
assessment or a request for more information within ten business days. These
are operational targets, not a guaranteed response service level.

Relevant report data is accessible only to the people handling the incident.
It is retained only as needed for resolution, review, and prevention of
repeated harm.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may remove or edit content, close interactions, restrict access, or
apply temporary or permanent bans. A response is selected according to impact,
urgency, repetition, intent, cooperation, and the safety of affected people.
Typical responses progress through:

1. Private guidance or correction for a low-impact first incident.
2. A formal warning with explicit boundaries and consequences.
3. A temporary restriction from project spaces and communication.
4. A permanent ban for severe harm or a sustained pattern of violations.

The sequence is a guideline, not a requirement to repeat unsafe intermediate
steps. Severe threats, harassment, disclosure of private information, or
retaliation may justify immediate restriction.

Moderation decisions must document the evidence considered, the policy basis,
and the action taken. Public explanations must not reveal confidential report
details or the identity of a reporter who requested privacy.

## Conflicts of Interest and Review

A maintainer who is the subject of a report, directly involved in the incident,
or otherwise conflicted must not decide the outcome. An available unconflicted
maintainer handles the report and any review.

A reporter concerned about the usual project contact may use
[GitHub's abuse-reporting process](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam)
without first sharing details through a conflicted channel.

If no unconflicted maintainer is available, the project cannot promise an
independent internal review. This limitation must be disclosed rather than
presenting a conflicted decision as independent.

An affected person may request one review of an enforcement decision through
the private contact. An available unconflicted maintainer considers whether the
evidence, process, and response were reasonable under this policy.
